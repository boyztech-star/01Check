Sharing files online should be simple. But between email size limits, compressed image quality, cloud storage permissions, and the endless parade of "please sign up to view this file" prompts — it often isn't.

### 2. Zip Multiple Files Before Sharing

If you need to share more than one file, compress them into a single ZIP archive before uploading. Most operating systems support this natively:

- **Windows:** Right-click → Send to → Compressed (zipped) folder
- **Mac:** Right-click → Compress
- **Android/iOS:** Use a free app like Files by Google or Zarchiver

Uploading one ZIP is always faster and cleaner than uploading files individually — and your recipient gets everything in one download.

---

### 3. Use QR Codes for In-Person Sharing

If you're in the same room as your recipient — at a meeting, classroom, or event — QR codes are the fastest transfer method available.

TempShare generates a QR code for every uploaded file. Your recipient just points their phone camera at it and the download starts. No typing URLs, no AirDrop compatibility issues, no Bluetooth pairing.

---

### 4. Reduce File Size Before Uploading

Smaller files upload faster, download faster, and are less likely to hit size limits. A few quick wins:

- **PDFs:** Use a free tool like Smallpdf or ilovepdf.com to compress before sharing
- **Images:** Save as WebP instead of PNG for significantly smaller files with no visible quality loss
- **Videos:** Use HandBrake (free, desktop) to re-encode with H.264 before sharing — often reduces size by 60–80% with minimal quality loss
- **Office documents:** Remove embedded fonts and unused slides/sheets before saving

---

### 5. Don't Use Email for Files Over 10 MB

Email attachments are technically limited to 25 MB at most providers — but in practice, anything over 10 MB risks being blocked by spam filters or the recipient's mail server.

Instead, upload the file to TempShare and paste the link in your email body. Your email is smaller, delivers faster, and doesn't clog anyone's inbox storage.

---

### 6. Use Platform-Specific Tools for Ongoing Collaboration

Temporary file sharing tools are great for one-off transfers, but for ongoing project collaboration use the right tool for the context:

- **Work documents:** Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 (editing + version history)
- **Design files:** Figma (collaborative design, browser-based)
- **Code:** GitHub or GitLab (version control + collaboration)
- **Team chat + files:** Slack or Microsoft Teams

The key is matching the tool to the use case — temporary tools for temporary needs, persistent tools for ongoing work.

---

### 7. Always Warn Recipients About Expiry Times

When you share a temporary link, tell your recipient how long they have to download it. Most people don't read the fine print on a download page.

A simple message like: *"Here's the file — it'll expire in 48 hours so grab it soon"* prevents the frustration of a broken link later.

With TempShare, the download page shows a live countdown timer, so even if you forget to mention it, your recipient can see the deadline themselves.
